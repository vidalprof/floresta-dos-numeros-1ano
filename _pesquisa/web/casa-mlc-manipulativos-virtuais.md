MLC Number Pieces is highly effective for visual learning when paired with direct adult instruction, but it will not teach your child math independently. While The Learning Standard has not yet formally evaluated this tool, its design aligns perfectly with best practices for cognitive scaffolding in early elementary mathematics.

MLC Number Pieces uses an open-ended constructivist approach by providing virtual manipulatives without rigid pathways or automated instruction. The interface presents a blank workspace where students drag units (ones), rods (tens), and flats (hundreds) onto the screen to build visual representations of numbers. The core learning mechanic relies on the grouping and ungrouping tools. If a student needs to subtract 15 from 32, they can place three tens and two ones on the board, select one ten-rod, and click the ungroup button to shatter it into ten individual ones. This explicit visualization directly models the cognitive process of regrouping. The app also includes a drawing tool, enabling users to circle groups, write standard algorithmic equations alongside their blocks, or label arrays. Because it is an open workspace, there are no levels to unlock or mastery checks to pass. Users clear the board and start fresh for each new problem provided by their textbook, teacher, or parent.

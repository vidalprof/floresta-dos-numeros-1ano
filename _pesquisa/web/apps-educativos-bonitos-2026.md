Before diving into specific recommendations, it is worth establishing what separates a genuinely educational app from one that merely claims the label. A child who loves dinosaurs gets reading activities about dinosaurs. It sounds simple, and it is, but the execution is excellent.

How much screen time is appropriate for kids using educational apps?

The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends no screen time for children under 18 to 24 months (except video calls), and for children 2 to 5, limiting to one hour per day of high-quality programming. For children 6 and older, consistent limits on time spent and ensuring it does not take the place of adequate sleep, physical activity, and other behaviors essential to health. Educational apps do not change these guidelines. The goal is quality and intentionality, not simply choosing a "learning" label. Even excellent apps are best used in bounded sessions with a parent nearby for younger children.

Are free educational apps actually as good as paid ones?

For general early learning, yes. Khan Academy Kids specifically is as good as or better than most paid alternatives on curriculum quality, engagement, and learning outcomes. The free tier is not a demo; it is the full product. The paid apps that outperform it do so on specific features, personalization in Homer's case and breadth of arts and science content in ABCmouse's, that may matter more or less depending on your child's needs. If you are unsure, start free and only pay when you have a clear reason to.

Which apps are best for phonics and early reading specifically?

For a free option, Khan Academy Kids has a solid phonics curriculum for pre-K through early elementary. Among paid apps, Starfall Education has the longest track record as a teacher-trusted phonics resource and is the most affordable paid option at around $35 per year. Reading Eggs offers the most structured phonics progression and best progress reporting, though at higher cost. Homer is the best option if your child resists structured phonics practice, since wrapping lessons in their interests meaningfully improves uptake. Avoid apps that call themselves reading-focused but do not have a systematic phonics sequence. Letter recognition alone is not reading instruction.

What are the best options for bilingual kids or families raising bilingual children?

Duolingo for Kids is the most accessible starting point for introducing a second language in an engaging format. It supports Spanish, French, Mandarin, and several other languages. For families doing full bilingual education, apps alone are not sufficient. Consistent spoken exposure and community remain far more important than any app. That said, Duolingo works well as a supplement to maintain the minority language between immersion moments. Khan Academy Kids is English-only, which is a real limitation for bilingual households. ABCmouse has some Spanish content but is not designed as a bilingual curriculum. Of the five apps not in our main reviews, Endless Alphabet is worth mentioning for vocabulary in English and works well alongside a language-learning app for dual-language exposure at the early ages.
